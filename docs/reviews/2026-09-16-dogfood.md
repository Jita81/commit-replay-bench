## 1. Verdict

**The instrument works on its own code, and it says the honest, uncomfortable things about it.** `[measured]` Registered with the product's own census-invariant setup (`pip -e .[server,dev]` + `uninstall commit-replay-bench` + `PYTHONPATH=<worktree>/src`), the probe went green in 1.1 s; **28 candidate commits were examined, 27 were RED at the parent (0 skips — every coupled commit we write is a real oracle), 26 were gold-checked and 23 (88 %) are gold-clean**; the three that are not were refused for the right reasons (two are our own ruff debt at the commit — RUF001, and an unformatted `mine.py` — one is code that lives outside `src/` so the gold overlay cannot reproduce it). The **negative controls gate passed — 14 tasks, 98 rows, 0 violations** — with **one measured oracle escape** (`hardcode_cheat` graded clean on `196fdb66`: its target test cannot tell the implementation from a six-entry lookup table — an oracle finding the product reports rather than hides, consistent with that task's mutation score of 0.625), and the **mutation oracle scored 10/10 tasks, mean strength 0.675** (5 strong ≥ 0.80, 3 adequate, 2 weak) — so the product would already route a third of our own cells `human` on oracle strength, and the two weak ones are exactly the commits a reviewer would flag (a 7-file "batch" whose single target test never touches six of the files; a boundary function whose tests pin no boundary). What the measurement also shows is **why a real campaign on this repository would be slow and mostly `calibrate`**: our test layout is one flat `tests/` directory, so the documented `AFFECTED_DIRS` belt is the entire suite (**18 min** per run — two runs per candidate at qualification), our targets are whole 1,000-line test files that spawn nested pytest/git/docker (a single RED/gold cycle costs 1–5 min; the oracle 12–224 s per task at 8 mutants), our commits are **S/M-heavy with almost no XS** (mean source churn 96 lines; 6 XS in the entire 288-commit history), the path taxonomy collapses 23 of 26 tasks into `bug.fix` even though 10 of them are `feat:` commits, and belt 5 sees only `ruff` where our own CI also gates on `mypy --strict`, `import-linter`, the code-map header check and 70 % coverage. Nothing here is a capability claim about any builder — no builder was run — but it is a `[measured]` statement that the pipeline mine → RED/baseline/gold → oracle → controls closes on our own repository at $0 with false-Q1 = 0 by construction, and a clear list of what to change so it measures us well.

## 5. The three most valuable changes to our repository so the product measures it well

1. **Split `tests/` into per-package directories and keep test files small.** `[measured]` The flat layout makes `AFFECTED_DIRS` the whole 18-minute suite and every target a 600–1,300-line file (≈ 5 min for `test_worker.py`), which sets the price of every belt run, every mutant and every builder test turn; it also makes the `regression` control not-constructible. `tests/core/…`, `tests/server/…`, `tests/builders/…` with one file per module gives a belt of 2–4 minutes `[hypothesis]`, a per-mutant cost of seconds, and a constructible regression poison.

2. **Commit hygiene: one behaviour per commit, coupled to its test, no batches.** `[measured]` 9 of 105 coupled commits are ≥ 20-file squashes, 12 of the last 60 commits are; 46 of 288 are merges the miner cannot use; the two weak oracles (0.00, 0.25) are a 7-file "batch" and a boundary function with no boundary test; 46 src-only commits (19 in `core`) carry no oracle at all and are invisible to the bench. The XS cell — the only cell the product has ever routed `deliver` — has 6 members in our whole history. Small, coupled, single-intent commits (`feat(x): …` + `tests/x/test_x.py`) are what the instrument can measure, and what the routing rule can promote.

3. **Make the oracle strong and the gates the same ones the belt runs.** `[measured]` Mutation scoring names the missing assertions (boundaries in `learn.rows_to_clear_bar`, `mutant_wall_clock`'s guard, FastAPI `Query` bounds, `learn.py` rounding); adding those tests raises strength from 0.25–0.63 to ≥ 0.80 and turns `human` routes into candidates. Pin `ruff` (`ruff==0.16.7` in `[dev]`) so belt 5 and CI agree by construction, run `ruff format` pre-commit so maintainers' commits stop failing their own gate (2 of 20), classify `src/crb/core/test_infra.py` correctly (rename or exempt `test_` source modules in `is_test_path`), put `scripts/` under `src/` (or register `src_prefix=''`), and — cheapest of all — **derive the intent label from the Conventional-Commit type at mine time** (`feat:`/`fix:`/`refactor:` are on 233 of 242 commits): it is $0, deterministic, and would split the degenerate `bug.fix` class into `feat`/`fix` cells without an LLM labeller.
